About

I study how information is encoded in signals, with a current focus on human speech.

My research asks how emotional, linguistic and physiological information can be characterised and separated within a single acoustic signal. I combine signal processing, information theory and machine learning, with applications in accent fairness in automatic speech recognition and quantitative clinical speech assessment.

I am a Lecturer in Mechatronics at Deakin University, Deputy Director of the Networked Sensing and Biomedical Engineering Lab, and Graduate Research Coordinator in the School of Engineering. My PhD research examined quantitative assessment of speech in cerebellar ataxia.

Engineering should improve lives, teaching should build capability, and leadership should enable both. This principle guides my research, teaching and work with students.
