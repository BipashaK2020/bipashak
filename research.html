Current research

Dimensional encoding in speech

Speech carries several kinds of information through the same acoustic channel. My work investigates statistical relationships between emotional, linguistic and physiological dimensions, using mutual-information estimation and source-filter analysis.

Accent fairness in automatic speech recognition and clinical speech assessment provide complementary settings for evaluating whether methods generalise across speakers and tasks.

With Bjorn W. Schuller and Pubudu N. Pathirana

PhD research & subsequent publications

Quantitative speech assessment

My research on cerebellar ataxia examines objective measures of speech timing and vocal characteristics. This includes topographic prominence for syllable detection, magnitude- and phase-based cepstral analysis, and models of changes in speech over time.

With Pubudu N. Pathirana, Malcolm Horne, Laura Power and David Szmulewicz

Quantum learning for voice assessment

With Ha Tran and Pubudu N. Pathirana, I investigated hybrid quantum-classical neural networks for dysphonia assessment in small speech datasets, comparing these approaches with convolutional models.

Learning from sensor data

Related work explores time-series image encoding, deep-learning ensembles and federated learning for sensor systems. These projects connect signal representation with practical constraints such as limited datasets and unreliable model updates.

Future directions

Filtering and controlled synthesis

I am interested in filters that reduce unwanted confounds while preserving task-relevant information, and in testing separation through controlled speech resynthesis. These are proposed research directions building on the dimensional-encoding work.
