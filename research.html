Project 1 · Completed PhD research, 2017–2021

Dysarthria and speech-motor assessment in cerebellar ataxia

Developing objective, quantitative measures of speech-motor impairment to complement clinical assessment. My doctoral work combined acoustic and cepstral features with machine-learning models of speech characteristics and change over time.

Methods include topographic prominence for syllable detection, magnitude- and phase-based cepstral analysis, and mixed-effect modelling of longitudinal recordings. Subsequent collaborative publications extend the work to movement assessment.

With Pubudu N. Pathirana, Malcolm Horne, Laura Power and David Szmulewicz. Doctoral support: CSIRO Data61 scholarship.

Project 3 · Current research

Spectro-temporal characterisation of dimensional encoding

Investigating where emotional, linguistic and physiological information is represented within speech, and how strongly these dimensions are statistically related.

I combine mutual-information estimation with source-filter analysis. The methods include MINE and CLUB bounds, a non-parametric KSG cross-check, and cepstral attribution. Accent fairness in automatic speech recognition and clinical speech assessment are complementary application domains.

The Interspeech 2026 paper reports an initial framework; broader spectro-temporal characterisation remains ongoing.

With Björn Schuller and Pubudu N. Pathirana.

Project 4 · Proposed research

Information-theoretic dimensional filtering

Investigating filters that suppress accent- or emotion-related confounds while preserving information needed by a specified task.

Planned methods include cepstral liftering, mutual-information minimisation, variational decomposition and rate-distortion analysis. This direction builds on the dimensional-encoding work; it is not yet a completed research output.

Project 5 · Proposed research

Synthesis-based validation of the encoding framework

Testing separation methods through controlled speech resynthesis: changing one dimension and examining how the other dimensions respond.

Planned work combines WORLD-vocoder synthesis with acoustic fidelity measures and listening studies. Any participant study will require ethics approval before it begins.
